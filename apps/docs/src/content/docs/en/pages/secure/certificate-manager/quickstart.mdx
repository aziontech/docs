This guide instructs you through binding your first certificate of your own to a [Workload](/en/documentation/platform/workloads/) with [Certificate Manager](/en/documentation/platform/workloads/#certificate-manager).

- Upload a server certificate and its private key to Certificate Manager.
- Bind the certificate to your Workload.
- Confirm that Certificate Manager reports the certificate as active.

Three elements take part, and each one links to the next:

1. The **certificate** is a server certificate you upload to Certificate Manager with its private key. The API stores it with `type` set to `edge_certificate`. Its status stays `inactive` until you bind it to a Workload.
2. The **Workload** holds the certificate in its `tls.certificate` field and presents it for your domains. While that field is `null`, the Workload presents the Azion SAN certificate instead. That is Azion's own certificate, and it lists the hostnames it covers as Subject Alternative Names (SANs).
3. The **domains** of the Workload are the hostnames the certificate must cover. The API does not compare the certificate's names with the domains, so it accepts a certificate that does not cover them. The client's check of the names then fails.

An uploaded certificate protects no traffic until you bind it to a Workload. The Azion SAN certificate covers only the workload domain and the `azion.app` hostname. A Workload keeps presenting it for those two hostnames after you bind your own certificate. This guide changes only the `tls` settings of the Workload and keeps its domains, ports, and deployment as they are.

To have Azion request a certificate from Let's Encrypt and renew it for you instead, refer to [Request a Let's Encrypt certificate](/en/documentation/guides/application-security/tls-and-certificates/how-to-generate-a-lets-encrypt-certificate/). In Azion Console, the Workload's **Digital Certificate** field also offers the Let's Encrypt presets, described in [Certificates](/en/documentation/platform/workloads/certificate-manager/certificates/#lets-encrypt-certificate).

## Upload the certificate

A server certificate holds the certificate and its private key. Once saved, the private key cannot be read back from Azion Console or the API. You can upload the certificate alone or with its intermediate certificates.

<Tabs client:visible sharedStore="interface">

<Fragment slot="panel.console">

### Console

To upload the certificate in Azion Console:

<DocSteps>
  <DocStep title="Open the Certificate Manager page">

  Access [Azion Console](https://console.azion.com/) > **Certificate Manager**.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Start a new certificate">

  Select **Create Digital Certificate**. The **Create Digital Certificate** page opens.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Select the Server Certificate preset" />
  <DocStep title="Name the certificate">

  In **Name**, enter a name for the certificate, such as `my-certificate`.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Paste the certificate">

  In **Certificate**, paste the PEM certificate, including its `-----BEGIN CERTIFICATE-----` and `-----END CERTIFICATE-----` lines. The help text reads "Intermediate certificates are accepted."

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Paste the private key">

  In **Private Key**, paste the PEM private key, including its `-----BEGIN` and `-----END` lines.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Select Create" />
</DocSteps>

The certificate appears in the **Certificate Manager** list as a **TLS Certificate**. It stays `inactive` until you bind it to a Workload.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.cli">

### CLI

To upload the certificate with the Azion CLI, pass the paths of the two PEM files:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`azion create digital-certificate --name my-certificate \\
  --certificate server.pem --private-key server.key`} />

The command prints the ID of the new certificate:

```text
Created Digital Certificate with ID <certificate-id>
```

Record the ID to bind the certificate to your Workload. List the certificates of your account:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`azion list digital-certificate --details`} />

The output shows your certificate with the type `edge_certificate` and the status `inactive`:

```text
ID                NAME            STATUS    ISSUER  VALIDITY                   TYPE              MANAGED  LAST EDITOR   LAST MODIFIED
<certificate-id>  my-certificate  inactive          2026-01-31 12:00:00+00:00  edge_certificate  false    <your-email>  2026-01-01 12:00:00.000000 +0000 UTC
```

The certificate exists in Certificate Manager, and no Workload uses it yet.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.api">

### API

To upload the certificate with the API, send a `POST` request to the certificates endpoint. `type` set to `edge_certificate` makes it a server certificate. In the body, `certificate` and `private_key` are JSON strings. Write each one as a single continuous string that keeps its `-----BEGIN` and `-----END` lines, with every line break written as `\n`. Replace `[TOKEN VALUE]` with your personal token:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`curl --request POST \\
  --url https://api.azion.com/v4/workspace/tls/certificates \\
  --header 'Accept: application/json' \\
  --header 'Authorization: Token [TOKEN VALUE]' \\
  --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \\
  --data '{
  "name": "my-certificate",
  "type": "edge_certificate",
  "certificate": "-----BEGIN CERTIFICATE-----\\n<certificate-body>\\n-----END CERTIFICATE-----\\n",
  "private_key": "-----BEGIN PRIVATE KEY-----\\n<private-key-body>\\n-----END PRIVATE KEY-----\\n"
}'`} />

The API answers with `201` and returns the new certificate. Record its `id` to bind the certificate to your Workload. The certificate exists in Certificate Manager, and no Workload uses it yet.

</Fragment>

</Tabs>

## Bind the certificate to your Workload

A Workload presents a server certificate for your domains only once you bind it in the Workload's `tls.certificate` field. Before you bind it, check that the certificate covers the domains of the Workload. The API accepts a certificate that does not cover them, and the client's check of the names then fails.

<Tabs client:visible sharedStore="interface">

<Fragment slot="panel.console">

### Console

To bind the certificate in Azion Console:

<DocSteps>
  <DocStep title="Open the Workloads page">

  Access [Azion Console](https://console.azion.com/) > **Workloads**.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Open your Workload">

  Select your Workload. The **Edit Workload** page opens.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Turn on HTTPS support">

  In **Protocol Settings**, turn on **HTTPS support** if it is off. **Digital Certificate** appears only while it is on.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Select your certificate">

  In **Digital Certificate**, select your certificate in the **My certificates** group.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Select Save" />
</DocSteps>

Azion Console shows the message "Your workload has been updated". Your certificate is bound to the Workload in `tls.certificate`.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.cli">

### CLI

To bind the certificate with the Azion CLI, save this JSON as `tls.json`, with your IDs in place of the placeholders:

<Code client:visible lang="json" code={`{
  "id": <workload-id>,
  "tls": {
    "certificate": <certificate-id>,
    "ciphers": 7,
    "minimum_version": "tls_1_3"
  }
}`} />

The file sends the whole `tls` object. It sets your certificate and keeps the defaults of a new Workload: cipher suite `7`, and TLS 1.3 as the minimum version. If your Workload uses other values, send those instead. Send the file:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`azion update workload --file tls.json`} />

The command prints the ID of the Workload:

```text
Updated Workload with ID <workload-id>
```

Your certificate is bound to the Workload in `tls.certificate`.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.api">

### API

To bind the certificate with the API, send a `PATCH` request to your Workload with the `tls` object. It sets your certificate and keeps the defaults of a new Workload: cipher suite `7`, and TLS 1.3 as the minimum version. If your Workload uses other values, send those instead. Replace `<workload-id>` and `<certificate-id>`:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`curl --request PATCH \\
  --url https://api.azion.com/v4/workspace/workloads/<workload-id> \\
  --header 'Accept: application/json' \\
  --header 'Authorization: Token [TOKEN VALUE]' \\
  --header 'Content-Type: application/json' \\
  --data '{
  "tls": {
    "certificate": <certificate-id>,
    "ciphers": 7,
    "minimum_version": "tls_1_3"
  }
}'`} />

The API accepts the update, and your certificate is bound to the Workload in `tls.certificate`.

</Fragment>

</Tabs>

## Confirm the certificate is active

Certificate Manager reports a certificate as `active` once you bind it to a Workload. The status shows that your Workload uses the certificate.

<Tabs client:visible sharedStore="interface">

<Fragment slot="panel.console">

### Console

To confirm the status in Azion Console:

<DocSteps>
  <DocStep title="Open the Certificate Manager page">

  Access [Azion Console](https://console.azion.com/) > **Certificate Manager**.

  </DocStep>
  <DocStep title="Find your certificate">

  Find your certificate in the list, and confirm that its status is `active`. A `pending` certificate shows a warning icon, and a `failed` certificate shows an error icon with the reason.

  </DocStep>
</DocSteps>

Your certificate is active, and your Workload uses it.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.cli">

### CLI

To confirm the status with the Azion CLI, list the certificates of your account:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`azion list digital-certificate --details`} />

The output shows your certificate with the status `active`:

```text
ID                NAME            STATUS  ISSUER  VALIDITY                   TYPE              MANAGED  ...
<certificate-id>  my-certificate  active          2026-01-31 12:00:00+00:00  edge_certificate  false    ...
```

Your certificate is active, and your Workload uses it.

</Fragment>

<Fragment slot="panel.api">

### API

To confirm the status with the API, send a `GET` request to your certificate. Replace `<certificate-id>`:

<Code client:visible lang="bash" code={`curl --request GET \\
  --url https://api.azion.com/v4/workspace/tls/certificates/<certificate-id> \\
  --header 'Accept: application/json' \\
  --header 'Authorization: Token [TOKEN VALUE]'`} />

The response returns your certificate. These are some of its fields, with `status` set to `active`:

```json
{
 "id": <certificate-id>,
 "managed": false,
 "name": "my-certificate",
 "status": "active",
 "type": "edge_certificate"
}
```

Your certificate is active, and your Workload uses it.

</Fragment>

</Tabs>

Binding a certificate is a change to the Workload. The change takes several minutes to reach all of Azion's distributed infrastructure, with no guaranteed duration. Meanwhile, requests can meet the old configuration or the new one. For more information, refer to [Propagation](/en/documentation/platform/workloads/how-it-works/#propagation).
